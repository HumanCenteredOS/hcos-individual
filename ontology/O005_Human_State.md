# O-002 — HCOS™ Human States

**Document ID:** O-002  
**Category:** Ontology — Human States  
**Status:** Draft  
**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Last Updated:** August 2026

---

## Purpose

Human States describe conditions a person may currently be experiencing.

They answer:

> What condition is the person currently experiencing?

States change over time.

They should never be treated as permanent identity or inherent worth.

---

## Rested

A condition in which sufficient recovery has occurred to support participation, attention, judgment, and engagement.

---

## Overwhelmed

A condition in which current demands exceed available capacity.

Overwhelm may reflect system pressure rather than individual weakness.

---

## Burned Out

A condition of sustained depletion, disconnection, reduced capacity, and diminished ability to continue under chronic system demands.

Burnout should not be reduced to an individual coping failure.

---

## Hopeful

A condition in which the person can perceive meaningful possibility, support, change, or future movement.

---

## Connected

A condition of meaningful relationship with self, others, community, purpose, reality, or source.

---

## Disconnected

A condition in which meaningful relationship, belonging, presence, identity, or contact with reality feels reduced or inaccessible.

---

## Integrated

A condition in which identity, experience, emotion, reality, meaning, and action can be held together with relative coherence.

---

## Fragmented

A condition in which experience, identity, attention, emotion, meaning, or action feels divided, disconnected, or difficult to hold together.

Fragmentation may be protective, temporary, or caused by sustained pressure.

---

## Recovering

A condition of active restoration following harm, depletion, loss, illness, disruption, or sustained pressure.

Recovery may require protection, support, time, meaning, and reduced demand.

---

## Flourishing

A condition in which the person is experiencing meaningful capacity, connection, participation, purpose, growth, recovery, and dignity.

---

## Grieving

A condition of responding to loss, change, absence, rupture, or unrealized possibility.

Grief is not dysfunction.

---

## Afraid

A condition of perceived threat, danger, uncertainty, vulnerability, or anticipated harm.

Fear may contain important information.

---

## Curious

A condition of openness to learning, exploration, possibility, and understanding.

Curiosity often requires sufficient safety and capacity.

---

## Safe

A condition in which the person experiences sufficient protection from harm, coercion, humiliation, instability, or threat.

Safety may be physical, emotional, relational, cognitive, professional, spiritual, or environmental.

---

## Unsafe

A condition in which harm, threat, coercion, instability, humiliation, or loss of control is present or reasonably anticipated.

---

## Suffering

A condition of pain, deprivation, loss, disconnection, threat, injustice, fragmentation, or unmet human need.

Suffering may be:

- physical;
- emotional;
- cognitive;
- relational;
- spiritual;
- organizational;
- social;
- systemic.

Suffering should be understood, not used as an identity label.

---

## Depleted

A condition in which available capacity has been significantly reduced.

---

## Supported

A condition in which sufficient relational, structural, material, or practical support is available.

---

## Isolated

A condition of reduced access to meaningful relationship, support, recognition, or belonging.

---

## Heard

A condition in which voice has been received, understood, and treated as meaningful.

---

## Silenced

A condition in which voice is suppressed, dismissed, punished, ignored, or rendered ineffective.

---

## Meaningful Use

Human States may be used to:

- describe current experience;
- identify support needs;
- recognize change;
- guide protection and recovery;
- help AI systems avoid treating temporary conditions as identity.

They should not be used to:

- rank human worth;
- diagnose without authority;
- predict character;
- justify punishment;
- erase context;
- assign blame.

---

## Temporal Principle

A Human State should include time and context whenever possible.

Example:

```text
Person
experienced
Overwhelm

during
High Workload Period
```

This is more accurate than defining the person as “an overwhelmed person.”
