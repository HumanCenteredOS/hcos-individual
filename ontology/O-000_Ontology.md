**Document ID:** O-000

**Status:** Draft  
**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Last Updated:** August 2026

## Purpose

The HCOS™ Ontology defines the shared language used throughout Human-Centered Operating Systems™ and related Human Based Operating Systems™ implementations.

The ontology provides a consistent vocabulary for describing:

- people
- systems
- relationships
- experiences
- change
- healing
- stewardship
- flourishing

Every Principle, Foundation, Method, Reality Engine, AI Agent, Instrument, and Operating System should reference the ontology rather than creating new or conflicting definitions.

---

## Why the Ontology Exists

As HCOS grows, similar concepts may begin to appear across:

- Foundations
- Disciplines
- Standards
- Methods
- Instruments
- AI Agents
- Human Maps
- Reality Engines
- assessments
- learning resources

Without a shared ontology, the same term may be used differently in different places.

The ontology creates a common conceptual layer so HCOS can:

- remain coherent;
- support consistent interpretation;
- represent relationships explicitly;
- preserve meaning across repositories;
- enable knowledge-graph reasoning;
- support AI agents without relying only on free text;
- distinguish temporary states from permanent identity;
- observe change without reducing people to scores.

## Ontology Categories

The ontology is organized into seven documents.

### 1. Core Objects

Describes entities, conditions, and concepts that exist within the HCOS model.

Core question:

> What exists?

### 2. Human States

Describes the current condition a person may be experiencing.

Core question:

> What condition is the person currently experiencing?

### 3. Human Dynamics

Describes movement, transition, or activity within and around the person.

Core question:

> What is happening?

### 4. System Dynamics

Describes what a human system is doing.

Core question:

> What is the system doing?

### 5. Relationships

Defines typed connections among ontology concepts.

Core question:

> How are these concepts connected?

### 6. Human Map

Represents the person’s current lived configuration.

Core question:

> What is the person’s current state within the surrounding system?

### 7. Measures

Defines observable dimensions of change without turning people into scores.

Core question:

> What can be observed over time?

## Ontology Principles

### Human Dignity Is Inherent

No state, measure, capacity level, diagnosis, role, or system classification determines human worth.

### State Is Not Identity

A person may be overwhelmed, fragmented, disconnected, or afraid without those conditions defining who they are.

### Capacity Changes

Capacity may change over time, across domains, and in response to suffering, support, recovery, environment, and meaning.

### Relationships Matter

Human experience is shaped not only by isolated concepts but by how pressure, support, protection, truth, voice, identity, connection, and systems interact.

### Systems Influence States

Human states should not automatically be interpreted as individual failure. They may reflect workload, policy, leadership, resource allocation, exclusion, loss, trauma, or system pressure.

### Measures Should Support Understanding

Observation should help people recognize needs, protections, change, and recovery. It should not be used to rank human worth.

### Meaning Must Be Preserved

Definitions should remain understandable to people while also being structured enough for AI and knowledge systems.

## Ontology Relationships

The ontology may be represented as a knowledge graph.

Example:

```text
Pressure → decreases → Capacity
Support → increases → Capacity
Protection → enables → Voice
Voice → reveals → Suffering
Truth → refines → Perception
Connection → supports → Integration
Healing → restores → Capacity
Stewardship → protects → Flourishing
```

This allows HCOS systems to reason over:

- entities;
- states;
- dynamics;
- relationships;
- change over time;
- provenance;
- context;
- uncertainty.

---

## Ontology Governance

New terms should not be added merely because they are useful in one document.

Before adding a term, ask:

- Does the concept already exist?
- Is this a synonym or a genuinely distinct concept?
- Is it an object, state, human dynamic, system dynamic, relationship, or measure?
- Is the definition clear?
- Does it apply across HCOS?
- What existing terms does it relate to?
- Could the term be misunderstood or used to label people?
- Does adding it strengthen or complicate the ontology?

---

## Ontology Stewardship

The ontology should be reviewed for:

- duplicate concepts;
- category confusion;
- inconsistent definitions;
- missing relationships;
- terminology drift;
- inaccessible language;
- unintended labeling;
- evidence gaps;
- changes in HCOS architecture.

---

## Architectural Principle

> Healthy knowledge systems distinguish what exists, what is happening, what the system is doing, how concepts relate, and how change is observed.
