# O-003 — HCOS™ Human Dynamics

**Document ID:** O-003  
**Category:** Ontology — Human Dynamics  
**Status:** Draft  
**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Last Updated:** August 2026

---

## Purpose

Human Dynamics describe movement, transition, or activity within and around a person.

They answer:

> What is happening?

Dynamics may increase, decrease, interrupt, restore, fragment, connect, or transform human experience.

---

## Pressure

The movement of demands, burdens, threats, expectations, or constraints toward or through a person.

Pressure can accumulate, transfer, concentrate, or be reduced.

---

## Support

The provision or movement of resources, relationship, protection, understanding, time, or practical assistance toward a person.

---

## Hustle

Sustained effort driven by urgency, scarcity, pressure, extraction, fear, survival, or the need to prove worth.

Hustle may produce short-term movement while depleting long-term capacity.

---

## Flow

A condition of coordinated action in which capacity, purpose, timing, resources, skill, and environment are sufficiently aligned.

Flow is not constant ease. It reflects reduced unnecessary friction.

---

## Extraction

The removal or use of human effort, time, attention, identity, knowledge, emotion, or capacity without sufficient protection, reciprocity, recovery, recognition, or consent.

---

## Recovery

The restoration of capacity, regulation, connection, function, safety, meaning, or participation after demand, harm, depletion, or disruption.

---

## Learning

The development of understanding, skill, pattern recognition, judgment, or changed action through evidence, experience, reflection, and feedback.

---

## Adaptation

A change in response to altered conditions.

Adaptation may be healthy or harmful.

A person adapting to dysfunction does not make the dysfunction healthy.

---

## Reality Shift

A change in the person’s understanding of reality following new evidence, experience, truth, loss, betrayal, insight, or perspective.

Reality shifts may temporarily reduce integration or capacity.

---

## Growth

The development of capacity, understanding, identity, relationship, judgment, skill, purpose, or participation.

Growth should not be forced through preventable suffering.

---

## Repair

The process of addressing rupture, harm, misunderstanding, loss of trust, broken relationship, or system failure.

Repair may require:

- truth;
- accountability;
- protection;
- listening;
- restitution;
- changed behavior;
- time;
- renewed trust.

---

## Meaning Making

The process of interpreting experience and integrating it into identity, purpose, reality, and future action.

---

## Connection

The movement toward meaningful relationship, belonging, presence, recognition, or shared reality.

---

## Fragmentation

The movement by which experience, attention, identity, emotion, relationships, or meaning become divided or disconnected.

---

## Integration

The movement by which previously separated or conflicting experiences, truths, identities, emotions, or meanings become more coherently held.

---

## Healing

The movement toward restored capacity, connection, safety, identity, meaning, and participation.

---

## Voice Emergence

The process by which a person becomes able to recognize, express, and trust their own experience or truth.

---

## Capacity Depletion

The reduction of available physical, emotional, cognitive, relational, spiritual, or practical capacity.

---

## Capacity Restoration

The rebuilding of capacity through protection, support, rest, connection, meaning, healing, resources, and reduced pressure.

---

## Dynamic Principle

A dynamic should describe movement rather than identity.

Example:

```text
Chronic Pressure
contributes to
Capacity Depletion
```

not:

```text
Person
is
Weak
```
