# O-001 — HCOS™ Core Objects

**Document ID:** O-001  
**Category:** Ontology — Core Objects  
**Status:** Draft  
**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Last Updated:** August 2026

---

## Purpose

Core Objects define the primary entities, conditions, and concepts that exist within HCOS™.

They answer:

> What exists?

Core Objects should not be confused with temporary states, actions, or system processes.

---

## Human Being

A person with inherent dignity, identity, experience, capacity, relationships, needs, agency, and potential for flourishing.

A Human Being is never reducible to:

- performance;
- diagnosis;
- productivity;
- role;
- capacity;
- behavior;
- system usefulness.

---

## Human System

A connected arrangement of people, relationships, roles, workflows, policies, resources, decisions, technologies, culture, and meaning.

Examples include:

- families;
- organizations;
- care systems;
- communities;
- schools;
- governments;
- teams.

---

## Reality

The conditions, events, relationships, structures, and experiences that exist, whether or not they are fully understood.

Reality may be:

- directly experienced;
- partially observed;
- interpreted differently;
- hidden by power;
- clarified through truth and understanding.

---

## Perception

The person’s or system’s interpretation of reality.

Perception may be influenced by:

- experience;
- fear;
- trauma;
- identity;
- information;
- power;
- trust;
- culture;
- capacity;
- relationships.

Perception is meaningful but may remain incomplete.

---

## Capacity

The current ability to carry, process, respond to, participate in, or recover from demands.

Capacity may include:

- physical;
- emotional;
- cognitive;
- relational;
- spiritual;
- practical;
- moral;
- creative capacity.

Capacity changes and does not determine worth.

---

## Dignity

The inherent worth of every human being.

Dignity is not earned through productivity, compliance, health, intelligence, status, behavior, or usefulness.

---

## Identity

The person’s understanding and expression of who they are.

Identity may be supported or constrained by:

- relationships;
- culture;
- power;
- safety;
- voice;
- belonging;
- truth;
- meaning.

---

## Voice

The ability to express experience, needs, truth, perspective, concern, preference, and identity.

Voice requires more than permission to speak. It also requires conditions in which expression can be heard, understood, and acted upon safely.

---

## Connection

The state of meaningful relationship with self, others, community, reality, purpose, or source.

Connection may support:

- trust;
- belonging;
- integration;
- healing;
- meaning;
- resilience.

---

## Integration

The degree to which experience, identity, reality, emotion, meaning, and action can be held together coherently.

Integration does not require the absence of complexity or contradiction.

---

## Support

A resource, relationship, condition, protection, or action that helps a person or system carry pressure, recover capacity, understand reality, or move toward flourishing.

---

## Pressure

A demand, force, burden, threat, expectation, or condition that requires capacity.

Pressure may be:

- temporary or chronic;
- visible or hidden;
- internal or external;
- necessary or unnecessary;
- distributed fairly or transferred unjustly.

---

## Protection

A condition, boundary, resource, relationship, or system function that reduces preventable harm and preserves dignity, capacity, voice, judgment, or recovery.

---

## Purpose

A meaningful direction, reason, responsibility, or contribution that helps organize action and identity.

Purpose should not be used to justify exploitation or endless sacrifice.

---

## Truth

That which reflects reality with integrity.

Truth may require:

- evidence;
- honesty;
- listening;
- context;
- correction;
- humility;
- courage.

---

## Understanding

A developing ability to connect facts, context, relationships, experience, meaning, and consequence.

Understanding is deeper than information alone.

---

## Compassion

The capacity to recognize suffering, remain connected to the humanity of those affected, and respond in ways that reduce harm without abandoning truth or accountability.

---

## Faith

Trust or commitment that allows a person to continue toward meaning, relationship, truth, or possibility when full certainty is unavailable.

Faith should not require denial of reality.

---

## Discipline

The sustained alignment of action with truth, purpose, responsibility, and long-term flourishing.

Discipline is distinct from punishment or forced compliance.

---

## Healing

The restoration or development of capacity, connection, integration, safety, identity, meaning, and participation following suffering, fragmentation, harm, or depletion.

Healing may be nonlinear.

---

## Flourishing

A condition in which people and systems possess meaningful opportunities for dignity, connection, participation, growth, purpose, health, contribution, recovery, and belonging.

Flourishing is not perfection.

---

## Excluded From Core Objects

The following belong elsewhere in the ontology:

- Leadership — System Dynamic
- Governance — System Dynamic
- Repair — Human or System Dynamic
- Extraction — Human or System Dynamic
- Hustle — Human Dynamic
- Flow — Human Dynamic
- Suffering — Human State or experienced condition
- Recovery — Human Dynamic
- Learning — Human and System Dynamic
